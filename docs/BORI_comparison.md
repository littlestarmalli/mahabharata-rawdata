# Mahabharata — BORI Critical Edition vs This Translation

## What is BORI?

The **Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (BORI)** in Pune produced the *Critical Edition* of the Mahabharata between **1919 and 1966** (without Hari Vamsha; complete with Hari Vamsha by 1970). A board of scholars collated all major regional versions (Bengal, Bombay, South) and eliminated later interpolations to produce an authenticated, standardised text.

This translation by **Bibek Debroy (Penguin Books)** follows the BORI Critical Edition exclusively.

## Key Facts

- The Mahabharata is **~4× the size of the Ramayana** and the longest epic in the world
- The BORI critical edition is **~26% shorter** than the traditional 1,00,000 shloka claim
- The text was composed over **~1,200 years** (800 BCE – 400 CE), with multiple authors adding layers
- **Footnote numbering restarts** at each sub-parva section (the 100-parva sections, not the 18-parva)
- The **two narrators** are: Vaishampayana (reciting at Janamejaya's snake-sacrifice) and Lomaharshana/Souti (reciting at the Naimisharanya gathering)
